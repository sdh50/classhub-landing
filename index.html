What happened to Canvas

In May 2026, Instructure — the company behind Canvas — permanently shut down its Free-for-Teacher program after a security breach that exploited a vulnerability in that exact system, exposing names, emails, and student IDs across thousands of schools.

If you were one of the individual teachers who relied on that free account to run your own class, there's no official replacement from Canvas. Classhub is what we built instead — in an actual classroom, for an actual gradebook, before we ever thought about sharing it.

Built for the gap other tools leave open

vs. Canvas & Blackboard

No district contract needed

Canvas and Blackboard are sold to districts, not teachers — an individual can't just sign up. Classhub is yours from the start, whether or not your district provides an LMS.

vs. Google Classroom

One tool, not three

Classroom's quizzes aren't built in — they're bolted on through Google Forms, and then you check results in a third place entirely. In Classhub, quizzes are built in from the start, with the question-by-question breakdown living right next to the gradebook.

Every class, one view

See your whole roster at a glance

One page shows every student, across every class period you teach, with their current grade. Assign each student to their actual class period (1st, 2nd, 3rd...) and filter the gradebook down to just that group whenever you need to.

vs. Google Classroom

Upload your Canvas export (.imscc) and your modules, assignments, and quiz questions come in as-is. Google Classroom has no course-import feature at all — teachers rebuilding from Canvas do it one assignment at a time, by hand.
